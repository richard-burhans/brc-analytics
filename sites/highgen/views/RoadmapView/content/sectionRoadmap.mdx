      ## Our plan
      <SubHeadline>
        Every public Cannabis sativa genome in one place, ready to analyze in Galaxy
      </SubHeadline>
    </SectionHeadline>
    <SectionContent paired>
      <Grid gridSx={{ gap: 4 }}>
        <Accordion>
          <AccordionSummary>★ Upcoming features</AccordionSummary>
          <AccordionDetails>
            Specific features planned for upcoming releases:

            1. Catalog the assemblies that have no NCBI accession, such as the Salk pangenome on figshare and deposits in Zenodo, NGDC, CoGe, Ensembl Plants and GigaDB
            2. Mark duplicate records of the same assembly across repositories, so each distinct genome is counted once
            3. Add Cannabis sativa to the Galaxy reference data cache, so read-based workflows (RNA-seq, ChIP-seq, ATAC-seq, Hi-C) can run on these assemblies
            4. Offer Galaxy workflows for comparative genomics across the cannabis panel: masking, whole-genome alignment, chaining and pangenome graphs
            5. Link each assembly to its BioProject, sequencing runs and publications
          </AccordionDetails>
        </Accordion>
        <Accordion>
          <AccordionSummary>Cultivar and chemotype context</AccordionSummary>
          <AccordionDetails>
            Cannabis research is organized around cultivars and chemotypes. We intend to:

            1. Record cultivar, sex and chemotype for each assembly where the source states it
            2. Let users browse and filter assemblies by those attributes
            3. Keep the provenance of every attribute visible, so a stated value is never confused with an inferred one
          </AccordionDetails>
        </Accordion>
        <Accordion>
          <AccordionSummary>Lowering barriers to bioinformatics</AccordionSummary>
          <AccordionDetails>
            In order to make complex analyses accessible to researchers with varying computational expertise, we will:

            1. Leverage Galaxy's GUI-based platform and pre-built workflows, eliminating the need to learn command-line interfaces
            2. Run analyses on the public usegalaxy.org service, removing the need for institutional or paid hardware
            3. Integrate curated, community-developed Galaxy workflows from the Intergalactic Workflow Commission (IWC)
            4. Build on the Galaxy Training Network's tutorials to provide educational resources
          </AccordionDetails>
        </Accordion>
      </Grid>
